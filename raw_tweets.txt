链接: https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/26/1149923/the-next-generation-of-bionic-hands-is-here-and-they-can-feel/
正文: Avi Davidson lost his left hand as a teenager. Since then, he’s cycled through a few prosthetics. But his latest is different—it’s wired up to nerves in the upper part of his forearm and allows him to feel pressure. For the first time in years, he can use his left limb to handle delicate objects like grapes, eggs, and a loved one’s hand. Davidson, who is taking part in a clinical trial run by the University of Utah, is the ninth person to have this experimental device implanted in his arm. But he’s the first to take it home and use it independently. The hand is part of the bionic LUKE arm, named after Luke Skywalker of Star Wars, who was fitted with a lifelike prosthetic arm after his own was light-sabered off. The arm was developed in the 2000s, initially trialed in 2014, and approved by the FDA that same year. The trial that Davidson is enrolled in isn’t so focused on the arm itself but on a new way to connect it to a person’s nervous system. The connection allows users to control movements and receive feedback about how much pressure they’re applying. Researchers hope that Davidson’s experiences will help them answer some long-standing questions about the technology, including how useful it really is in day-to-day life. They want to know when the device is helpful and where it might fail. They also want to learn how it might affect Davidson’s well-being as well as his brain and nervous system. The loss of a limb In 2009, when he was 16, Davidson climbed a telephone pole to get the best shot of a sunset. “I climbed about 35 feet up,” he recalls. But he lost his footing as he tried to descend. He grabbed for a wire, which ended up electrocuting him, before he fell to the ground. The accident resulted in second- and third-degree burns over 37% of his body, says Davidson, “and I broke my back in approximately 50 separate locations.” He also broke eight ribs, and his spinal cord was severed.
