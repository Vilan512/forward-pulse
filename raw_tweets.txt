Much of the back-end data that’s generated—from billions of daily prompts, images, and videos—needs to be stored. But data storage is one of the harder infrastructure costs to plan for, and it’s often overlooked in initial AI infrastructure planning. As inference scales, the data footprint grows with it Traditionally, AI infrastructure conversations have centered on the compute-intensive process of training a model. Training is finite: a run starts, burns through compute, and ends. But inference runs continuously, every time a model answers a prompt. For infrastructure planning, that difference matters: A training run is a project you can budget for and retire. Inference doesn’t retire; every inference request leaves something behind. Take AI-generated video. In this workload, the back-end spend can be significant. From internal measurements, WD found that an 8-second clip (returned to a user as a 3.97MB 720p file) left more than 500MB of associated data behind on the back end, including logs, cached context, immediate renders, backups, and safety records. That’s a 130x increase over the output file size the user saw. Scale this to an enterprise generating longer, higher resolution video and the back-end footprint grows into the hundreds of gigabytes per clip. And that back-end footprint is growing fast.
