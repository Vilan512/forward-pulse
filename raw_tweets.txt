But he was already thinking about AI risk. The previous day, the AI nonprofit METR had released a report that had been widely interpreted as evidence that Anthropic had achieved recursive self-improvement, a phenomenon in which AI meaningfully contributes to its own advancement and AI progress potentially spins out of control. “It’s very concerning,” Wiener said, citing the report as evidence that we are quickly running out of time to regulate AI. “It was my hope that Congress would take decisive action before [the end of] this year,” he said. “That does not appear to be happening.” Wiener has been increasingly focused on AI in recent years, and he’s made many of the AI safety movement’s concerns his own: During our 20-minute conversation, he used the phrase “recursive self-improvement” half a dozen times, for example.“We need to slow this down,” he says. “Because it’s moving very quickly, and we could sleepwalk into a disaster.” Wiener has already had real influence on AI policy. SB 1047, a bill that he authored in 2024, was set to become the first real piece of frontier AI legislation in the country before it was vetoed by California governor Gavin Newsom; he then sponsored SB 53, a lighter-touch bill that Newsom signed. Both bills have inspired proposed federal legislation.&nbsp; Wiener’s congressional campaign received over $3 million from Public First Action, a nonprofit in favor of AI regulation that receives substantial funding from Anthropic. That contribution led opponents to criticize him for being too close to the AI industry, but Wiener says that couldn’t be further from the truth.&nbsp;

For specialty chemicals manufacturer Lubrizol, digital transformation is all about giving researchers the tools to test ideas faster, make better-informed decisions, and develop more sustainable materials without compromising performance. Girish Rao, vice president of information technology at Lubrizol, says the company is building a digital foundation that connects business processes, modern data platforms, and cloud infrastructure to support its research and development (R&amp;D) teams. Their goal is to combine decades of accumulated technical data with the deep expertise of chemists and engineers who understand customer needs as well as the physical constraints of their work. Data scientists can help identify patterns, test new hypotheses, and model possibilities before researchers conduct physical experiments. But, Rao emphasizes, “it&#8217;s not about replacing scientific judgment; it is about giving our scientists better tools.” Scientists, data scientists, and IT teams approach problems from different perspectives, prioritizing experimental validity, model performance, and platform security and scalability, respectively. By bringing these groups together in “fusion teams,” Lubrizol aligns them around shared objectives, starting with small pilots to build trust. &#8220;Once the trust is built between the team members and they know what value they&#8217;re about to create,&#8221; Rao says, &#8220;things go a lot better.&#8221; Cloud infrastructure also gives the company flexibility to access computing resources and modern tools without making the substantial investments required to build and maintain its own data centers. These capabilities could prove especially valuable in advancing sustainability.
