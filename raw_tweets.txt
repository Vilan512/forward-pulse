The company’s goal, says founder Alex Wiltschko, is “to give computers a sense of smell.” “Scent molecules are really the only language most species on this planet can speak,” he says. “Most species can’t communicate with sound or visually, so it truly is Earth’s original language. And it’s incredibly powerful for us, from a memory and an emotional standpoint. You can smell something once and remember it for the rest of your life. You can smell something on the street and be pulled back to high school.” In the last few months, Wiltschko claims, Osmo’s model has achieved “human-level olfactory intelligence.” The company’s definition of this is that the model is about as good as the average person at detecting and discriminating between smells, and about as good as a junior perfume designer at creating them. It says its technology is already being used in the fragrance industry to help brands design and manufacture new scents. Last month, the company says, Osmo’s AI model outperformed in-house perfumers at one of the world’s largest consumer goods companies.
