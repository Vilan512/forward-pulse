But an extreme, prolonged drought in the Colorado River Basin has resulted in dangerously low water levels in the Southwest’s two largest reservoirs, affecting the availability of this power source. Residents and farmers in the US Southwest, a region that includes Arizona, New Mexico, and parts of Nevada and California, rely on water that melts in the spring from snowpack in the mountains and is slowly allowed to flow south via dam releases. Snowfall in the Rocky Mountains provides about 85% of the annual flow of the Colorado River. And in recent years, the river has seen record-low flows.&nbsp; Two reservoirs along the Colorado River, Lake Powell and Lake Mead, have struggled to stay above the minimum level required for hydropower plants. The Hoover Dam power plant requires 950 feet in Lake Mead, and that reservoir was 9% above the minimum in September.
